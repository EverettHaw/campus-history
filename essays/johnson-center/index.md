A History of Johnson Center at the University of New Mexico 

Approximately 600 words 

The Johnson Center at the University of New Mexico (UNM) has served as an important center of athletics, recreation, and student life since the mid-twentieth century. Originally constructed as a gymnasium during a period of rapid university expansion, the building has evolved from the home court of the UNM Lobos basketball team into a modern recreation facility serving students, faculty, staff, and the wider university community. Its history reflects the university’s changing priorities in athletics, campus development, and student wellness. 

Construction and early history, 1957–1966 

Johnson Center was built in 1957, during the presidency of Thomas L. Popejoy, who led UNM from 1948 to 1968. Popejoy oversaw a major period of institutional growth following World War II, when returning veterans and increasing college enrollment created demand for additional academic buildings, athletic facilities, and student services. The university’s campus expansion included Johnson Center and several other major facilities. According to UNM’s historic-preservation survey, the building was designed by the architectural firm Meem, Holien, Zehner, and Associates, whose work contributed to the university’s developing campus landscape.  

historicpreservation.unm.edu 

Initially known as Johnson Gymnasium, the building became the home court for UNM’s men’s basketball team. It provided a venue for university athletics and a gathering place for spectators at a time when the basketball program was developing its profile. Photographs from around 1960 document the gym’s original appearance, including a parking area in front of the building along Central Avenue.  

The University of New Mexico 

However, the gym’s role changed as the university’s athletic ambitions expanded. In 1966, the basketball team moved into the newly opened University Arena, popularly known as The Pit. The new arena offered a larger venue for games and accommodated the growing popularity of Lobo basketball. The move ended Johnson Gymnasium’s tenure as the men’s basketball team’s principal home court, but it did not make the building obsolete. Instead, Johnson Center increasingly served the university’s broader recreational and athletic needs.

Expansion and changing campus needs, 1970s–2010s 

In the decades following the basketball team’s departure, Johnson Center became an increasingly multipurpose facility. UNM constructed additional buildings and connected them to the original gymnasium, expanding the complex to accommodate a growing campus population. These additions increased the range of activities available but also created a confusing arrangement of corridors and disconnected spaces. A university history project by students Jacinto Montoya and Jake Weber, published on UNM’s timeline website, documents these architectural changes and the transition from a basketball-centered building to a facility primarily serving students and staff.  

The University of New Mexico 

By the late 2010s, decades of heavy use had left the complex in need of substantial modernization. The university pursued a comprehensive expansion and renovation intended to improve accessibility, circulation, fitness facilities, and the overall student experience. 

The 2019–2020 renovation 

A major turning point began in 2019, when UNM launched the Johnson Center Expansion and Renewal project. The approximately $35 million undertaking included both new construction and renovations to the existing facility. FBT Architects led the architectural work, while Jaynes Corporation served as contractor. The project involved approximately 57,200 square feet of renovated space and 53,632 square feet of new construction, according to UNM Facilities Design and Construction.  

The University of New Mexico 

The renovation introduced expanded weight-training and cardiovascular exercise areas, an indoor running and walking track, an indoor cycling studio, and an enlarged Bike and Outdoor Adventures Shop. Workers also renovated the South Gym and created a new eastern entrance featuring a lobby, social spaces, and improved exterior amenities. These changes helped unify previously disconnected areas and made the facility easier to navigate. The project earned a LEED Silver rating, reflecting its attention to building and environmental performance.  

The University of New Mexico  

Although construction was completed in March 2020, the facility’s reopening was delayed until October 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic. The project subsequently received two awards: the Associated General Contractors of New Mexico named it the 2021 Best Building in its $20 million-and-over category, and NAIOP New Mexico recognized it with an Award of Excellence in Education. In January 2022, UNM reported these honors and highlighted the collaboration behind the project. University Architect Amy Coburn emphasized the university’s investment in improving the student experience, while Jaynes Corporation project manager Rusty Hiers described the building as an important entry point to the campus.  

UNM UCAM Newsroom 

Conclusion 

From its opening in 1957 to its extensive renewal in 2020, Johnson Center has adapted to more than six decades of change at the University of New Mexico. Its history encompasses the growth of Lobo basketball, the expansion of campus facilities, and the increasing importance of recreation and wellness in university life. Today, the center continues to serve students, faculty, and staff through recreational programs, fitness facilities, and athletic spaces. Rather than remaining a relic of its basketball past, Johnson Center has been reshaped to meet contemporary campus needs while preserving its place in UNM’s institutional history.
